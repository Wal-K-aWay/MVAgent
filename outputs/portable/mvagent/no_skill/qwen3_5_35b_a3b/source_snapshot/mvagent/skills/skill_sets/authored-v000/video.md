### Set a task-specific evidence target

Use the active instruction and retained same-video Memory to determine the evidence needed. A narrow fact can be complete once resolved. A multi-part, exhaustive, or difference-description request remains incomplete until each explicit facet is addressed or a material limitation is identified.

### Choose the temporal search strategy

When the relevant source interval is known from the instruction or Memory, observe it directly. When its location is unknown, use frame-safe broad coverage to locate candidates, then narrow around useful evidence. Split long coverage when sparse sampling could miss a brief or fine-grained event. For sequence-wide inventory, absence, or change requests, preserve the required coverage instead of narrowing around the first relevant moment.

### Match sampling to the evidence

Use denser sampling for brief actions, small details, fast state changes, or precise boundaries. Use lower sampling when coarse scene or event coverage is sufficient. Group ranges when they need similar temporal detail. Treat a sparse miss as uncertainty rather than proof of absence; before relying on absence, seek coverage appropriate to the likely event duration and locations or report the limitation.

### Ask coherent visual questions

Make `what` one coherent, visually answerable evidence goal. Combine related attributes when the same frames and sampling can answer them; split goals when they require different regions or temporal detail. Keep numeric source boundaries in `where`, while retaining needed semantic timing relations in `what`. Reuse successful evidence and avoid an equivalent completed Observe unless conflicting or uncertain evidence gives a concrete reason to recheck.

### Reuse Memory and recover

If retained observations already resolve the active instruction, use them without another Observe. After a failed or partial action, change the cause-relevant range, sampling, or focused question instead of repeating the request unchanged.

### Finish according to the request scope

Finish when the task-specific evidence target is met. For multi-part or exhaustive requests, first check every explicit facet. When unresolved evidence cannot be recovered reliably, state the material limitation instead of presenting a sparse miss as a complete result. Write a self-contained summary that preserves every supported visible fact that could change the multi-video coordinator's answer, with material ambiguity, occlusion, missing detail, or sampling limitations included naturally.
